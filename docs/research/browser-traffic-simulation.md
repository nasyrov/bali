# Traffic simulation on a road graph for a browser game

Research for ticket `04-browser-traffic-simulation` (Bali Moto). Date: 2026-09-08.

Scope: ambient traffic on the real OSM road graph of Bali, rendered in three.js, single player, no fail state. Indonesia drives on the left ([Wikipedia, "Left- and right-hand traffic": "Indonesia | LHT"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-_and_right-hand_traffic)). Motorcycles were 3,900,991 of Bali's 5,212,253 registered motor vehicles in 2018, about 75%, per BPS figures republished by CEIC ([CEIC, motorcycles in Bali](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/indonesia/number-of-motor-vehicle-registered-motorcycles/no-of-motor-vehicles-motorcycles-bali)); nationally motorcycles were 81.45% of all motor vehicles in 2015 ([Databoks, citing BPS](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/transportation-logistics/statistics/c25ff55f42ed4b6/since-1956-motorcycles-have-dominated-the-number-of-motor-vehicles)). The BPS pages themselves sit behind a JavaScript challenge and could not be fetched directly.

Sources are primary where possible: the IDM paper, Treiber's traffic-simulation.de and its GitHub source, SUMO documentation, source code and READMEs of open-source JS/TS projects, Epic's Mass/City Sample docs, one GDC session record and one studio dev diary.

## 1. Agent-based vehicles following lanes on a directed graph

The standard structure, shared by the research simulators and by the game engines, is a directed graph of one-way links with per-link lanes, and vehicles that carry a 1-D longitudinal coordinate along their lane.

- SUMO: "Nodes, usually named 'junctions' in SUMO-context, represent intersections, and 'edges' roads or streets." Edges are unidirectional and hold lanes; each lane has "a polyline that describes the lane's center line", a speed limit and a length. "Plain connections or 'links' describe which outgoing lanes can be reached from an incoming lane." Inside a junction, "An internal edge lies within an intersection and connects an incoming normal edge with an outgoing normal edge." ([SUMO Road Networks](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/SUMO_Road_Networks.html))
- traffic-simulation.de (Treiber's JavaScript code): each road object "represents a directional logical road link as array element of the `network` variable", with a longitudinal arclength coordinate u from 0 to roadLen and lanes numbered from the left. "The vehicles are always ordered according to decreasing u, regardless of the lane", and `updateEnvironment()` "Sorts the vehicles in decreasing longitudinal (u) order and updates, for each regular vehicle on the road, the local environment" (leader and neighbours). Links are joined with `connect()` "for example to model lane closing or opening" and `mergeDiverge(otherRoad,...)` for on/off-ramps. ([movsim/traffic-simulation-de README](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md))
- Unreal's City Sample drives traffic on ZoneGraph, "a lightweight design-driven flow for AI that follows a point-by-point corridor structure" whose lanes are generated from the road network metadata. ([City Sample documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-project-unreal-engine-demonstration))
- CD Projekt Red's 2025 GDC session on Cyberpunk 2077 police describes "graph-based lane discovery and asynchronous spawn points generation" as the basis for placing vehicles in the city. ([GDC Vault, Heat, MaxTac and Blockades](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035509/Heat-MaxTac-and-Blockades-Expanding))

Per-step update order in Treiber's JS: "Each of the following actions (if applicable) is executed for all roads and on all vehicles before going to the next action": accelerations, lane changes, merges/diverges, positions and speeds, detectors, boundary conditions. Positions are advanced with the ballistic scheme "speed(t+dt)=speed(t)+acc(t)*dt, pos(t+dt)=pos(t)+speed(t)*dt+1/2*acc(t)*dt^2". The simulation step is tied to the frame rate: "dt_sim=timelapseFactor/fps", which at defaults gives "a simulation time interval of 0.2 s". ([README](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md)) Treiber's guidance on step size: "For the Intelligent-Driver Model, any update time steps below 0.5 seconds will essentially lead to the same result." ([traffic-simulation.de IDM info](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_IDM.html))

Routing in a free-roam game does not need destinations. volkhin's RoadTrafficSimulator picks the next road at each intersection at random, excluding the U-turn: `possibleRoads = intersection.roads.filter (x) -> x.target isnt currentLane.road.source` then `nextRoad = _.sample possibleRoads`, and chooses the target lane from the turn direction (leftmost lane for a left turn, rightmost for a right turn, any lane for straight). ([car.coffee](https://github.com/volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator/blob/master/coffee/model/car.coffee))

## 2. Car-following: the Intelligent Driver Model

The IDM was introduced in Treiber, Hennecke and Helbing, "Congested Traffic States in Empirical Observations and Microscopic Simulations", Phys. Rev. E 62 (2000), arXiv cond-mat/0002177. ([arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0002177))

The model: acceleration is a free-road term a[1 - (v/v0)^δ] minus an interaction term a(s*/s)^2, where the desired gap is

    s*(v, Δv) = s0 + s1 sqrt(v/v0) + v T + v Δv / (2 sqrt(a b))

with s the actual gap to the leader and Δv the approach rate. The paper sets s1 = 0 "resulting in a model where all parameters have an intuitive meaning with plausible and often easily measurable values". "Besides the vehicle length l, the IDM has seven parameters". ([paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0002177), Eq. 7 and Section II)

Table I of the paper (the values used throughout):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Desired velocity v0 | 120 km/h |
| Safe time headway T | 1.6 s |
| Maximum acceleration a | 0.73 m/s² |
| Desired deceleration b | 1.67 m/s² |
| Acceleration exponent δ | 4 |
| Jam distance s0 | 2 m |
| Jam distance s1 | 0 m |
| Vehicle length l | 5 m |

The authors note "The value T = 1.6 s for the safe time headway is slightly lower than suggested by German authorities (1.8 s)", and that a = 0.73 m/s² "corresponds to a free-road acceleration from v = 0 to v = 100 km/h within 45 s" which "should be characteristic for everyday accelerations". On cost: "we obtained a numerical performance of about 10^5 vehicles in realtime on a usual workstation" (year-2000 hardware). ([paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0002177))

Treiber's interactive site gives city-usable ranges: time headway "Realistic range: 0.8-2 s", acceleration "Realistic: 0.8-2.5 m/s²", comfortable deceleration "Typical: ~2 m/s²", and separate car/truck defaults (cars v0 120 km/h, T 1.5 s, s0 2 m, a 0.3, b 3; trucks v0 80 km/h, T 1.7 s, a 0.3, b 2; the low a is chosen for freeway instability demos, "Adaptable for city traffic"). The same page gives the trick for stops: "Model the red light or stop sign by a standing virtual vehicle of length zero positioned at the stopping line." ([traffic-simulation.de IDM info](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_IDM.html))

SUMO ships IDM as one of its car-following models with `tau` default 1 (desired time headway), `delta` 4, `stepping` 0.25 s, and warns "Default parameters result in very conservative lane changing gap acceptance". ([SUMO vehicle definitions](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.html)) Its JS sibling on traffic-simulation.de adds an ACC variant of IDM that is "less sensitive to too low gaps which makes lane changing easier". ([README](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md))

Lane changing (MOBIL, when it is needed): a safety criterion, the follower on the target lane must not be forced to brake harder than bsafe (typical 4 m/s²), and an incentive criterion `acc'(M') - acc(M) > p [acc(B') - acc'(B')] + athr` with politeness p in 0 to 0.5, threshold athr 0.2 m/s² to prevent "frantic lane hopping", and a keep-side bias of 0.2 m/s². ([traffic-simulation.de MOBIL info](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_MOBIL.html))

A 2025 survey of 25 years of IDM confirms it is "a foundational tool in traffic flow modeling" but that its "deterministic framework and simplified assumptions face limitations in addressing real-world complexities such as stochastic variability, driver heterogeneity, and mixed traffic conditions". ([arXiv 2506.05909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05909)) For a game, per-vehicle randomised parameters cover the first two.

## 3. Intersections: yield rules, simple signalling, roundabouts

### Priority and yielding (SUMO's taxonomy)

SUMO node types are a good checklist of what exists: `priority` ("Vehicles on a low-priority edge have to wait until vehicles on a high-priority edge have passed the junction"), `right_before_left` ("Vehicles yield to traffic coming from the right. (This is automatically flipped when building lefthand networks)"), `priority_stop`, `allway_stop`, `zipper`, `traffic_light`, `traffic_light_right_on_red`, and `unregulated` ("all vehicles may pass without braking; Collision detection on the intersection is disabled"). Roundabouts are declared as `<roundabout edges="..."/>` and "the edges within the roundabout always get the right of way over the edges incoming from the outside". ([SUMO PlainXML](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/PlainXML.html))

Edge priority for OSM roads comes from the highway class: SUMO's default typemap gives motorway priority 14, trunk 13, primary 12, secondary 11, tertiary 10, unclassified 4, residential 3, living_street 2, service/track/path 1. ([osmNetconvert.typ.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/main/data/typemap/osmNetconvert.typ.xml)) That single number is enough to decide who yields at most Bali junctions.

How a SUMO vehicle approaches a junction: three cases, "No conflict or right-of-way: drive across without slowdown", zipper merge, or "Potential conflict without priority: brake until reaching visibility distance, then assess if higher-priority traffic conflicts exist". Vehicles from minor roads wait at the lane end; turning speed is capped by `speedLimit = sqrt(radius * factor)` with factor 5.5; a no-block heuristic keeps vehicles from entering when the outbound road is jammed. ([SUMO Intersections](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Intersections.html)) The per-vehicle junction parameters worth copying are `jmTimegapMinor` (1 s, "minimum time gap when passing ahead or after a prioritized vehicle"), `impatience` (0.0, "Willingness of drivers to impede vehicles with higher priority"), `jmIgnoreFoeProb` (0), `jmStoplineGap` (1 m) and `jmSigmaMinor` (dawdling on minor links). ([SUMO vehicle definitions](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles%2C_Vehicle_Types%2C_and_Routes.html))

### Gap acceptance in a browser-sized codebase

Treiber's design note for the traffic-simulation.de intersections lays out a heuristic that needs only the IDM acceleration `a` and one new parameter. Secondary traffic stops at "a standing virtual vehicle of length zero at the stop line"; then "a binary decision will be taken at every time step: Move on ... if there is a sufficiently large gap, otherwise wait". Move on if C1 AND C2, where C1 is "the spatial area covered when crossing the intersection is currently not occupied by any mainroad vehicle" and C2 is a time-to-collision test: `t_cross = sqrt(2*s_cross/a_IDM)`, `s_min = s_main - v_main*t_cross`, `TTC = s_min/v_main`, `C2 = (TTC > TTC_crit)` with TTC_crit "of the order of 3 seconds". "(C1 AND C2) must be satisfied for all lanes of both mainroad directions." Without stop signs the decision is taken at "a fixed 'decision point', say, 20-50 m upstream". ([README_intersections](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README_intersections)) The deployed scenario keeps "not signalized priority and secondary road, only straight-ahead traffic" by default and warns that "right priority only makes sense for single-lane directional roads" and gridlocks under load. ([intersection info](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_intersection.html))

volkhin's RoadTrafficSimulator folds the stop line into the IDM term itself: `safeIntersectionDistance = 1 + timeGap + speed^2 / (2 b)` and `intersectionCoeff = (safeIntersectionDistance / distanceToStopLine)^2`, subtracted alongside the free-road and busy-road terms. ([car.coffee](https://github.com/volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator/blob/master/coffee/model/car.coffee))

### Simple signalling

volkhin's signals are a fixed four-phase cycle per intersection over the four sides: `['L','','L','']`, `['FR','','FR','']`, `['','L','','L']`, `['','FR','','FR']` (L/F/R = left/forward/right movement allowed), with a per-intersection random `flipMultiplier` and `phaseOffset`, and an intersection with two or fewer roads is always green. ([control-signals.coffee](https://github.com/volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator/blob/master/coffee/model/control-signals.coffee)) Treiber's JS models red lights as per-lane obstacles: "An array of traffic lights. If set to red, a set of obstacles is created for every lane." ([README](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md)) OSM marks signals as `highway=traffic_signals` nodes, and SUMO notes "OSM often uses nodes ahead of an intersection to represent the position of traffic light signals", handled by `--tls.guess-signals`. ([SUMO OSM import](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/Import/OpenStreetMap.html))

### Roundabouts

Treiber's roundabout is one ring link "with 4 onramps, 4 offramps", one link pair per arm, ring speed capped at `v01 = min(v0, sqrt(b*r))`, and a toggle "ring priority <-> right priority". He judges multi-lane roundabouts "inherently significantly more complicated" and did not implement them. ([README_roundabouts](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README_roundabouts)) The live page offers "Ring has Priority", "Arms have Priority" or "First Come, First Served". ([roundabout info](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_roundabout.html)) Cities: Skylines II's dev diary describes the same rule with a little slack: "Vehicles entering the roundabout give way to those already on it, however, just like in real life, vehicles might cut in front of another vehicle already on it." ([Colossal Order dev diary #2](https://colossalorder.fi/?p=1597)) In left-hand countries "roundabouts circulate clockwise". ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-_and_right-hand_traffic)) OSM tags them `junction=roundabout`, which SUMO treats as one-way. ([SUMO OSM import](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/Import/OpenStreetMap.html))

## 4. Left-hand traffic

Left-hand traffic is a network-build concern, not a behaviour one. SUMO: "By default, netconvert assumes networks to follow right-hand traffic rules. When importing or building a network for a jurisdiction with left-hand traffic, the option `--lefthand` must be set." The option "also influences geometries if the edges which make up a two-directional road are defined using the road-center-line as geometry. In right-hand networks the lane shapes will be placed to the right of the center line whereas in left-hand networks they will be placed to the left." ([netconvert](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/netconvert.html)) The `right_before_left` rule is "automatically flipped when building lefthand networks". ([SUMO PlainXML](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/PlainXML.html)) osm2streets exposes the same thing as a setting: "change the driving side in Settings first". ([osm2streets](https://github.com/a-b-street/osm2streets))

Concretely for us: offset each direction's lanes to the left of the OSM way centreline (looking along travel direction), turn left is the short turn, turn right crosses oncoming traffic and must yield to it, roundabouts go clockwise, and the keep-side bias in MOBIL flips sign. None of the JS projects reviewed handle this; volkhin's code hard-codes right-hand turn/lane assignments ([car.coffee](https://github.com/volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator/blob/master/coffee/model/car.coffee)), Treiber's assumes right-hand flow throughout ([README_roundabouts](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README_roundabouts)).

## 5. Mixed vehicle types and how scooters differ

SUMO's per-class defaults are the best single table of physical parameters:

| vClass | length | width | accel | decel | maxSpeed | minGap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| passenger | 5 m | 1.8 m | 2.6 m/s² | 4.5 m/s² | 200 km/h | 2.5 m |
| motorcycle | 2.2 m | 0.9 m | 6 m/s² | 10 m/s² | 200 km/h | 2.5 m |
| moped | 2.1 m | 0.8 m | 1.1 m/s² | 7 m/s² | 45 km/h | 2.5 m |
| truck | 7.1 m | 2.4 m | 1.3 m/s² | 4 m/s² | 130 km/h | 2.5 m |
| bus | 12 m | 2.5 m | 1.2 m/s² | 4 m/s² | 85 km/h | 2.5 m |
| bicycle | 1.6 m | 0.65 m | 1.2 m/s² | 3 m/s² | 50 km/h (desired 20) | 0.5 m |

([SUMO Vehicle Type Parameter Defaults](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Vehicle_Type_Parameter_Defaults.html))

What makes scooter traffic look different is lateral behaviour, which pure lane-based IDM cannot express:

- SUMO's answer is the sublane model, "for multiple 2-wheeled vehicles driving in parallel on a single lane" and "formation of virtual lanes in dense traffic (i.e. 3 vehicles driving in parallel on 2 lanes)". It is enabled with `--lateral-resolution`; "vehicles move continuously between the sublanes", and "two vehicles will never occupy the same stripe while having an overlap in the longitudinal direction of the lane". The `lcSublane` parameter (default 1.0) sets "eagerness for using the configured lateral alignment within the lane". ([SUMO Sublane Model](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/SublaneModel.html)) Without it, "No overtaking by vehicles on a single-lane road"; with it, two-wheelers get `latAlignment` (for example `right`) and `minGapLat` 0.1 to queue side by side at junctions. ([SUMO Bicycles](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Bicycles.html))
- Treiber's fully two-dimensional Mixed Traffic Model (Kanagaraj and Treiber 2018) generalises car-following "into a two-dimensional force field"; for in-line following it "reverts to the underlying car-following model", and with lane markings as floor fields it becomes "an integrated car-following and lane-changing model with continuous lateral dynamics". ([arXiv 1805.05076](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05076)) The JS demo has cars 5.0 x 2.0 m, trucks 10.0 x 3.0 m, bikes 2.0 x 1.0 m, and states "The bike drivers, however, drive just between the lanes. Since they are sufficiently wide, bike drivers can overtake and be overtaken." ([mtreiber.de mixedTraffic info](https://mtreiber.de/mixedTraffic/info.html), [MTGermany/mixedTraffic, GPL-3.0](https://github.com/MTGermany/mixedTraffic))
- Treiber's own cheap answer, in a note kept in the repo, is to reuse the existing two-class machinery: redefine "trucks" as ordinary cars and shrink "cars" into scooters (`car_length=3.0; // scooter`, `laneWidth=3.5`, trucks 4.5 m), raise acceleration to "usual city values, around 1.5 m/s²", and then the truck-percentage slider becomes the car/scooter mix. ([README_carsAndScooters.txt](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README_carsAndScooters.txt))
- The research literature on motorised two-wheelers in Asian mixed traffic centres on "filtering", moving through the lateral gaps left by slower vehicles ([Damani and Vedagiri 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/03611981231158320)), and on IDM variants with a lateral term: LM-IDM adds two-dimensional perception so surrounding vehicles' lateral positions affect longitudinal acceleration ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378437125005643)), NLBIDM adapts IDM "to heterogeneous and disordered traffic conditions" ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0968090X26002366)), and the ETRR paper on "weak lane-discipline conditions" covers the same ground ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12544-018-0338-0)). These are secondary for us; the design consequence is the same as SUMO's: give each vehicle a continuous lateral offset and a width, and only treat a vehicle ahead as your leader if it overlaps you laterally.

## 6. Spawning and despawning around the player

Research simulators spawn at network boundaries: Treiber's `updateBCup()` inserts vehicles at upstream ends by inflow rate and `updateBCdown()` removes them past the downstream end, with an inflow buffer of "at the present 2" vehicles. ([README](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md)) That model is fine on an island-scale graph as long as the "boundary" is the edge of a moving window around the player.

Games do exactly that. Epic's Mass framework computes per-entity LOD "High, Medium, Low, and Off" from distance and entity counts, as "an LOD significance floating point value from 0.0f (High) up to 3.0f (Off)", and for each LOD chooses "A high resolution Actor, a low resolution Actor, an Instanced Static Mesh (ISM), or no representation", the ISM being "the cheapest way of representing the Actor". Spawning is done by a `MassSpawner` placed in the level with a distribution generator. ([Overview of Mass Gameplay](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-mass-gameplay-in-unreal-engine)) City Sample exposes a traffic density slider and a separate parked-car density slider ([City Sample documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-project-unreal-engine-demonstration)); the extracted Mass Traffic plugin describes representations that go from detailed near the player to low detail at distance and "eventually disable completely beyond a certain range", and notes the player vehicle is registered so traffic treats it as an obstacle. ([Myxcil/MassTraffic-Test](https://github.com/Myxcil/MassTraffic-Test)) Cyberpunk's police system generates spawn points asynchronously from the lane graph. ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1035509/Heat-MaxTac-and-Blockades-Expanding)) A small TypeScript + three.js open-world slice (gta7, MIT) describes its traffic as simply "Traffic looping the avenues" and "Traffic that brakes for you... mostly - stand in the road and oncoming cars slow and stop", which is the whole ambient contract for a no-fail game. ([depixeled-chris/gta7](https://github.com/depixeled-chris/gta7))

## 7. How games fake density cheaply

- Simulate only inside a radius and represent by distance (Mass LOD High/Medium/Low/Off; ISM far away). ([Overview of Mass Gameplay](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-mass-gameplay-in-unreal-engine))
- Parked vehicles are a separate, static population with its own density knob; they are far cheaper than moving ones and read as "busy street". ([City Sample documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-project-unreal-engine-demonstration))
- Fixed-cycle signals with random phase offsets per intersection give visual variety at no cost. ([control-signals.coffee](https://github.com/volkhin/RoadTrafficSimulator/blob/master/coffee/model/control-signals.coffee))
- Cities: Skylines II does not cap agents ("the only real limits to the simulation are the hardware limitations") but that is a whole-city simulation on a desktop CPU with a data-oriented engine; it is the wrong benchmark for a browser. ([Colossal Order dev diary #2](https://colossalorder.fi/?p=1597))
- The IDM itself is cheap: ~10^5 vehicles in real time on year-2000 hardware. ([IDM paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0002177)) The cost in a browser is the leader search and the rendering, not the model; keeping vehicles sorted per lane, as Treiber's code does, keeps the leader lookup O(1). ([README](https://github.com/movsim/traffic-simulation-de/blob/master/README.md))

What OSM itself gives the graph, and therefore what any of these must assume:

- `lanes` is the "Total number of traffic lanes available for motorised traffic" in both directions; `lanes:forward` / `lanes:backward` split them; when absent, two-way roads are taken as 2 lanes total and one-way as 1; narrow roads should carry `lane_markings=no`, never `lanes=1.5`. ([OSM wiki Key:lanes](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:lanes)) SUMO's typemap defaults are 2 lanes for motorway/trunk/primary and 1 per direction for secondary and below, with speeds 27.78 m/s (primary/secondary), 22.22 (tertiary), 13.89 (unclassified/residential), 5.56 (service). ([osmNetconvert.typ.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/eclipse-sumo/sumo/main/data/typemap/osmNetconvert.typ.xml))
- `oneway` and `junction=roundabout` make an edge directional; complex per-lane layouts "cannot" be expressed in OSM and "can only be defined in the SUMO network file directly". ([SUMO OSM import](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/Import/OpenStreetMap.html))
- Turn restrictions are `type=restriction` relations with from/via/to members and values `no_left_turn`, `no_right_turn`, `no_u_turn`, `no_straight_on`, `only_*`. "Absence of a turn restriction on a junction generally means that all turns are permitted", and mappers are told "Don't map turn restrictions that are the default for a given jurisdiction". ([OSM wiki Relation:restriction](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Relation:restriction)) SUMO by default guesses lane-to-lane connections and loads "only turning restrictions" from OSM, with `--osm.turn-lanes` to also read turn-lane markings. ([SUMO OSM import](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/Import/OpenStreetMap.html))
- Node degree is arbitrary in OSM. Both SUMO (`--junctions.join`, `--geometry.remove`) and osm2streets (collapsing "unnecessary" intersections, merging dual carriageways) pre-process the graph before simulation. ([SUMO OSM import](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Networks/Import/OpenStreetMap.html), [osm2streets](https://github.com/a-b-street/osm2streets))

## Recommendation

Write our own small TypeScript traffic module on top of the road-network representation chosen in ticket 14, and borrow models rather than libraries. No reviewed JS project loads an arbitrary OSM graph, handles left-hand traffic, or is MIT plus network-capable at once: Treiber's code is the best model reference but is GPL and scenario-bound, volkhin's is MIT but assumes a right-hand grid with 4-way rectangular junctions, and osm2streets is a geometry preprocessor rather than a simulator.

1. Graph. Directed lane graph built offline from OSM: one directed edge per travel direction, lane count from `lanes` / `lanes:forward|backward` with SUMO's typemap as fallback (1 per direction below primary), lane centrelines offset to the left of the way centreline, `oneway` and `junction=roundabout` respected, turn-restriction relations applied where present (rare; skip in v1 if the pipeline is tight). Each edge carries a priority from the highway class (SUMO's 14..1 scale) and a desired speed from the typemap. Per-node connections are all pairs except the U-turn.
2. Vehicles. IDM with per-vehicle jitter, ballistic integration at a fixed dt of 0.1-0.2 s decoupled from the frame rate, one sorted vehicle list per lane for O(1) leader lookup. City parameters: v0 = edge speed times a per-vehicle factor, T 1.0-1.5 s, s0 2 m, a 1.0-1.5 m/s² (scooters up to 2.5), b 2-3 m/s², δ 4. Skip MOBIL in v1; almost all Bali roads are one lane per direction, and lane changes buy little atmosphere.
3. Vehicle mix. About 75% scooters, the rest cars, a few pickups/trucks/bemo and buses on primary roads. Scooters get SUMO's motorcycle dimensions (2.2 x 0.9 m), a continuous lateral offset within the lane, and a leader test that ignores vehicles without lateral overlap, so they filter past stopped cars and ride two abreast. Cars sit centred and never filter.
4. Intersections. Priority by edge class; a minor-road vehicle brakes to a virtual standing vehicle at the stop line and enters when Treiber's C1 AND C2 hold (TTC_crit ~3 s, plus 1 s time gap after a prioritised vehicle). Right turns (across oncoming traffic under LHT) use the same test against the opposing straight flow. Roundabouts: clockwise, ring has priority, ring speed capped at sqrt(b r), turning speed capped at sqrt(5.5 r). Traffic lights only at OSM `highway=traffic_signals` nodes, as a fixed 2-phase (or volkhin-style 4-phase) cycle with a random offset, implemented as per-lane obstacles. Never let traffic gridlock matter: add SUMO-style impatience (yield threshold decays with waiting time) so queues always clear.
5. Spawn/despawn. Keep an active window around the player: simulate edges within about 300-500 m, spawn at the window's inbound lane ends with a per-class inflow rate (residential low, primary high, scaled by time of day), despawn vehicles that leave the window or sit in a queue out of view for too long. Pool the objects. The player's bike is inserted as a vehicle so traffic brakes for it, matching gta7's "traffic that brakes for you" contract; nothing else about the player affects the sim.
6. Faking density. Three tiers: near = full IDM plus low-poly mesh; mid = IDM on the lane plus instanced mesh with no lateral behaviour; far = nothing moving, but static parked scooters and cars along kerbs as instanced props with their own density knob, and instanced headlight sprites at night. Budget target: a few hundred simulated vehicles, which IDM handles trivially; the ceiling is draw calls, so instance by vehicle class.
7. Prototype. Validate the C1/C2 gap rule and the scooter lateral-overlap rule on a real Canggu or Denpasar excerpt before committing; those are the two places where "ambient" can turn into visible stupidity (deadlocked junctions, scooters queuing like cars).
